Random-Entry Null Model: the Four USDJPY Candidates

2026-11-24. Phase 6, step 0. Development data only (holdout never loaded into a backtest), no strategy changes, no optimisation, no risk-setting changes, no orders.

#Verdict

Nothing here shows that the candidates beat random timing.

Against being long USDJPY for the same amount of time, no candidate does better (primary run: p = 0.23–0.98). EMA H1 does worse than 98% of random long exposure with the same trade structure. Over the full period, all four sit in the middle of the long-only null (p = 0.34–0.53). The candidates' profits are therefore what you'd expect from being long USDJPY during its rise, which confirms the Phase 6 diagnosis.

Against random timing of the same trades, with the same long/short mix, the evidence is weak. In the primary run, ADX H1 and ADX H4 sit in the top 4–6% (p = 0.041 and 0.064), and EMA H1 and EMA H4 are unremarkable (p = 0.63 and 0.26). After correcting for multiple comparisons, no result is significant in the primary run.

Exactly one result stands out: EMA H4 in the no-swap sensitivity run (p = 0.006 against random timing, 0.008 against long exposure). That run isn't a realistic cost model. It barely survives correction within its own group of 8 tests (Holm-adjusted 0.048) and fails across the whole study (0.14). With swap included, the same candidate is unremarkable (p = 0.26 and 0.54).

What this means for the research. The step-0 rule was: if the candidates' results fall inside the null's 5–95% range, trend research on this pair is not worth continuing. In the primary run:

  • Against long exposure: 3 of 4 candidates are inside that range, and EMA H1 falls below it, meaning worse than random long exposure.
  • Against random timing: 3 of 4 are inside it, and ADX H1 is at the 96th percentile, which doesn't survive correction.

So the crossover trend family on USDJPY shows no evidence of skill beyond market beta. These results don't justify continuing it on this pair.

#1. How the null model works

The new module is fxq.validation.nullmodel, with 9 tests.

  1. Rebuild each candidate's real target-position series from the saved results:
    • Out-of-sample: the walk-forward selections saved by the validation study, window by window, for both cost variants. This is the same construction as stitched_backtest.
    • Full period: default parameters, with the drawdown halt off, as in the baseline diagnostic.
  2. Check the rebuild before anything else. All 12 rebuilt series reproduce the saved results exactly: net P&L to within $0.000001, trade counts, and halt flags. So the null is compared against the candidates' genuine trades.
  3. Shift it at random. The series is circularly shifted (the end wraps to the start) by a random number of bars, at least 5% of the series length. This keeps exactly the number of position changes, every holding period, the long/short sequence and the total time in the market. Only the timing relative to price is destroyed.
  4. Two versions of the null:
    • Matched: the shifted series as it is. Is the timing better than random, given this direction mix?
    • Long-only: the shifted series with every position made long. Does the candidate beat simply being long USDJPY for the same amount of time? Wherever the original reversed directly from long to short or back, one flat bar is inserted so that each original trade stays a separate trade. On the real series this keeps 96–100% of position runs and 98–99.5% of exposure.
  5. Same simulation as the candidates. Every null series runs through the same engine, costs and risk rules: real rules for the out-of-sample runs (1% risk, 2% daily limit, 15% halt, rollover window) and the halt-off diagnostic for the full period. Stops are 2 × ATR(14) at each bar's own time, the formula every candidate uses.
  6. Number of runs: 1,000 per version for the primary run (out-of-sample, with swap), and 500 per version for the no-swap and full-period runs. That is 16,000 null backtests in total, with fixed seeds (12345 + 100 × setting + version).
  7. How results are expressed. The percentile is the share of null runs worse than the candidate. The p-value is (1 + null runs at least as good) / (1 + runs). This is the one-sided chance that random timing does as well, so a small value is evidence against random timing.

Checking that the null itself works, on synthetic prices in the tests:

  • A signal that knows the next 24 bars' direction beats every random shift (p = 1/61).
  • A signal with no information sits inside the null (0.05 < p < 0.95).

#6. Interpretation

  1. The candidates' profits are consistent with long USDJPY exposure. Random long positions with the candidates' own holding periods and time in the market earn as much. This confirms Phase 6 finding 2: all net profit came from long trades during the 2021–24 rise.
  2. There is at best weak, unconfirmed evidence of timing skill beyond a random placement of the same trades, mainly for the ADX variants. It doesn't survive correction, and in the primary run it is no better than long exposure.
  3. The H1 results are dominated by the risk rules. 99% of random structures also halt. The halt is a property of the trade structure combined with the risk rules, not of particular entries (Phase 6, Section 7).
  4. Recommendation: treat the EMA/ADX crossover family on USDJPY as not validated and not worth tuning further. Use this null model as the standard benchmark for any future hypothesis, H-A to H-C, in addition to the nine validation checks. A new strategy should have to beat both null versions, in the primary run, after correction.

#7. Limitations

  • The null is conditional on each candidate's own trade structure. It tests timing, not whether a different structure (holding periods, frequency) would do better.
  • The circular shift keeps the position series' own autocorrelation and creates one artificial boundary where the end wraps to the start. Shifts below 5% of the length are excluded to avoid near-copies of the original.
  • Long-only inserts one flat bar per direct reversal. A one-bar position squeezed between two reversals disappears; on the real series that is 0–4% of position runs.
  • Resolution: the smallest possible p-value is 0.001 with 1,000 runs and 0.002 with 500.
  • Swap inherits the static November 2026 snapshot approximation in every run that includes it. The no-swap runs understate real carry.
  • Selection bias: the candidates were selected from 39 runs, which biases the full-period comparisons toward significance. The out-of-sample runs are less affected.
  • Everything is on USDJPY development data, 2020–2025 (out-of-sample 2022–2024). Nothing here says how these or other strategies would behave in another market regime.
